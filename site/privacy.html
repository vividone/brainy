Privacy notice

Last updated 4 August 2026

By default Brainy collects nothing. Your child's answers, progress and report stay in the browser on your device and are never uploaded. During setup we ask whether we may collect anonymous usage data; the box starts unticked, and if you leave it that way nothing is ever sent. Nothing about your child ever leaves the tablet, on any plan.

This website is a separate thing from the app, and is treated separately: these pages use Google Analytics to count visits. The app does not, and cannot. See Google Analytics below.

Who we are, and the law we work under

Brainy is made by Fortbridge Technologies Ltd, a company registered in Nigeria. For the purposes of data protection law, Fortbridge Technologies Ltd is the data controller for everything described in this notice. You can reach us about anything on this page at brainy@fortbridge.app, and we aim to answer within seven days.

We work to the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA), which is the law that governs us. Brainy is also used by families following the British and American curricula, so where the UK GDPR or the American COPPA rules would give someone a stronger right than the NDPA does, we apply the stronger one rather than the minimum. It is simpler to hold one high standard than three different ones.

Brainy is built for children in Basic 1 to Basic 6 and their equivalents, roughly ages 5 to 12. Every one of them is a child in the eyes of all three of those laws. That is the reason for the central design decision described throughout this notice: the child has no account, and nothing about the child is sent to us at all. Where a law requires verifiable parental consent before a child's personal data may be processed, we do not rely on obtaining that consent — we avoid the processing.

What we collect

Never anything about your child. The account is yours, not theirs — a child has no account, no password and nothing to sign in to, and no version of Brainy will give them one. Beyond your own registration, nothing is collected unless you opt in.

The exception belongs to the grown-up rather than the child. When you set Brainy up you register: we store your email address, and your name and phone number if you give them. That is what your access is attached to, so it can be restored if this tablet is lost or replaced, and it is how a free place is held for a person rather than for whoever happens to be holding a code. It is the only reason it exists, and we write to you about your own access and nothing else.

If setup cannot reach us — no signal, or we are down — Brainy lets you carry on anyway and asks again in the grown-up area later, because an app sold on working offline should not refuse to start when it is offline. Nothing about your child is sent when it does reach us.

How signing in works

There is no password. You give us your email address, we send a six-digit code, and typing it back proves the address is yours — which is the same thing a password reset would have proved, with fewer steps and nothing to forget. That code works once and expires after fifteen minutes, and five wrong guesses kill it.

Once you are in, the device keeps a long random token so it stays signed in. Two things worth being straight about:

  • It is stored in the browser on that device, which means a script running on the page could read it. Brainy loads no third-party script anywhere in the app, and the security policy we publish with it forbids one — that is the protection, and we would rather state it than imply the token is safer than it is.
  • Each device gets its own, so signing out on a tablet you have lost does not disturb the one in your hand. Sign out in the grown-up area revokes that device immediately.

We keep a rough label for each signed-in device — "Chrome on Android", say — purely so you can tell them apart. It is not a fingerprint and cannot identify the device to anybody else.

What is stored on your device

So that the app can remember where your child got to, the following is saved in your browser's local storage — on your device only:

  • The first name and age you entered, and the class and curriculum you chose
  • Which questions have been answered, and how well, per skill
  • Coins, level, streak, badges, and the characters and pets collected
  • A short history of recent sessions, including questions answered incorrectly
  • Your settings, including the four-digit grown-up code

You can see all of it, export it as a file, remove one child, or delete everything — including anything shared with us — from the grown-up area. Clearing your browser data also removes it.

What leaves your device

Nothing, unless you choose one of these three things:

  • You export a backup file and move it somewhere yourself. The file holds your children's progress, your grown-up code and your access code, so treat it like the access code itself — anyone you send it to could restore it. It never holds the sharing id: consent stays with the device it was given on, so a restored tablet asks you again.
  • You send the anonymous summary described below, either once or weekly.
  • You write to us using the feedback form.
  • You claim a free place, enter a code, or buy a licence — which sends your email address and the code, and nothing else. See Sign-ups, licences and payments below.

With all of these switched off and untouched — which is the default — Brainy makes no network requests at all after the page has loaded. That is also why it works offline, and it stays true of the child-facing part of the app on every plan.

Anonymous usage data, if you opt in

If you tick the box during setup — or switch it on later in the grown-up area — Brainy sends us a small amount of information so we can tell whether it is working:

  • That Brainy was set up, and which curriculum and class you chose
  • That it was opened on a given day, and how many children use this device
  • For each quest: the subject, how many questions, how many were right, and how long it took
  • A weekly summary of which topics are scoring worst
  • A random code, generated at the moment you opt in, so one tablet is not counted twice

It never includes your child's name, their age, or anything they typed.

About that random code

We want to be straight about this rather than call it "anonymous" and leave it there. The code does not identify your child, is not linked to a name or an email, and cannot be resolved to a person. But because it stays the same between visits, it lets us see that the same device came back — and under Nigerian and European data protection law that makes it pseudonymous data, not fully anonymous data. That is precisely why we ask for your consent first rather than assuming it.

The code is created only when you opt in, and deleted the moment you opt out. Before you agree, there is no identifier in Brainy at all.

Changing your mind

Grown-up area → Settings → Share anonymous usage data. Switching it off stops all sending immediately, asks us to delete everything already received under your code, and then deletes the code from your device. Deletion happens first and on purpose: the code is the only handle on those records, so once it is gone nothing could find them again.

If your tablet is offline at that moment the sending still stops, but the earlier records will remain — they carry no name, no contact details and no date beyond the day, and nothing will ever be added to them. The app tells you which of the two happened rather than assuming. Delete everything in the same screen does the same erasure and additionally wipes the whole app back to a fresh install.

How we learn whether the app works

Because we collect nothing by default, the only way we find out that a question is badly worded or a topic is too hard is if you tell us. In the grown-up area you can read the exact summary, then copy it, send it once, or switch on Send it automatically each week. That switch is off until you turn it on, and you can turn it off again at any time.

That summary deliberately contains:

  • The curriculum and class, and rough bucketed counts (for example "100–249 questions")
  • Overall accuracy, and which of our topics had the lowest scores

It deliberately does not contain:

  • Any name, device identifier or account of any kind
  • Any dates or timestamps
  • Any question text, or anything your child typed

We are being careful about the difference between de-identified and anonymous. A persistent identifier plus a detailed history would still be personal data even with the name removed, because it describes one individual. The summary avoids that by carrying no identifier and no timeline at all. Nothing is sent automatically, and nothing is sent unless you choose to send it.

The feedback form

The grown-up area has a feedback form. Whatever you type in it is sent to us, along with the category you picked and — only if you tick the box — the anonymous summary. An email address is optional and used solely to reply to you. Please do not include your child's name or other personal details; we do not need them and ask you not to send them.

Sign-ups, licences and payments

Mathematics — your child's class and every earlier class as revision — is free permanently for everybody, and needs no card at any point. The other subjects are covered by a licence, and the first twenty families keep everything free for ever. Registering is what attaches any of that to you; paying only comes into it if you are not on a free place.

We email you three things and nothing else: your access code, a confirmation if you paid, and a warning about a week before a licence runs out. No newsletter, no offers, no reminders to play.

When you claim a place, enter a code, or buy a licence, we store:

  • Your email address, and your name and phone number if you chose to give them
  • Which plan you are on, when it started, and when it runs out
  • Your family access code, and how many tablets have used it
  • For a purchase: the amount, the currency, a payment reference and whether it succeeded
  • If you pay by bank transfer: the name on the sending account, the date, the reference you gave us, and the receipt you attached if you chose to attach one. We need those to find your payment on our statement. Only we can see them — they are never on a public address — and you can ask us to delete the receipt once your code has arrived.

We do not store card details — we never see them. Nothing about your child is attached to any of it: no name, no age, no answers, no progress. The link between a licence and a child is the code on your tablet, and it stops there.

Your access code is checked with us about once a week when the tablet is online, which also records that the tablet used it — that is how we notice a code being passed around a hundred families. If we cannot be reached, nothing changes and your child keeps playing.

You can remove the licence from a device at any time in the grown-up area, under Access. That closes the paid subjects on that tablet and cancels nothing — entering the code again reopens them.

Google Analytics — on this website, not in the app

This website — the pages you are reading now — can use Google Analytics to tell us which pages people actually read, so we know what is worth writing and what is being skipped. It is the ordinary business of running a website, and we would rather do it openly than guess.

Google Analytics sets a cookie and receives, for this website only:

  • Which pages you looked at, and when
  • Roughly where in the world you are, from your IP address, which Google truncates
  • Your device type, browser and screen size
  • Which site or search sent you here, if any

We have switched off Google's advertising features: no Google Signals, no ad personalisation, no remarketing audiences. Nothing collected here is used to advertise to you or sold to anybody, and we cannot see who you are from it.

The app has none of this. Brainy itself, at brainy.fortbridge.app/play, contains no Google script, no analytics service and no third-party code whatsoever — the build refuses to publish if one ever appears there. So nothing your child does is measured by anybody, which is the point. The grown-up dashboard is excluded too.

Opting out. Nothing here is required for the website to work, so you can block it without losing anything. Any tracker-blocking extension stops it, as does Google's own opt-out add-on; clearing your browser data removes the cookies already set, and blocking third-party scripts for this site prevents new ones. If you would rather not be counted at all, we would rather you blocked it than felt watched.

Third parties

The app contains no analytics service, no advertising, no tracking pixels, no third-party fonts and no embedded content. Usage data, summaries and feedback go to our own server and are not shared with anyone else.

Three third parties are involved, and not one of them on a child's tablet:

  • Google Analytics measure these website pages — see the section above. Not the app.
  • Resend deliver our email — your access code, a payment confirmation, and a warning before a licence runs out. They process your address in order to deliver those messages and for nothing else. We do not run a mailing list, and there is nothing here to unsubscribe from: every message is about your own access.
  • Paystack take the payment, and only if you buy a licence. You go to their page to enter your card details, and they hold those details and the receipt — we receive only your email address, an amount and whether it worked.

Each has its own privacy notice covering what they do with what they are given. None of them receives anything about your child, because nothing about your child exists on our side to send them.

Advertising

There is no advertising in Brainy, and there will not be any in future versions.

Children's safety

Brainy has no chat, no messaging, no user-generated content and no way for a child to communicate with anyone. There are no external links anywhere in the child-facing part of the app. The only place that can navigate away is the grown-up area, which is behind a code.

Hosting and where data is kept

The site is served as static files by Vercel, our hosting provider, who may keep standard server logs including IP addresses for security and reliability. We do not read those logs to build any profile, and they are not joined to anything above.

Anything you do choose to send — usage data, summaries, feedback — is stored in our own database and is not shared with, or sold to, anyone. No third-party analytics service is involved in any of it — Google Analytics runs on these website pages only, never in the app, and the two are never joined.

Where your data is processed

Our website, the app and our small server all run on Vercel, and our database is hosted by a managed Postgres provider. Their servers may be outside Nigeria and outside the UK, which means the little we hold — your email address, a licence, a payment reference — may be processed abroad.

Where that happens we rely on the safeguards the NDPA requires for transfers out of Nigeria, and on our contracts with those providers, which bind them to process data only on our instructions and never for their own purposes. Nothing about a child is included, because nothing about a child ever leaves the tablet in the first place.

If something goes wrong

If we ever suffer a breach that puts personal data at risk, we will report it to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission within 72 hours of becoming aware, as the NDPA requires, and we will tell the people affected directly where there is a real risk to them. We would rather say so plainly than manage it quietly.

The blunt version of what a breach could expose: a list of parents' email addresses and which licence each holds. Not one child's name, age, answer or score, because we do not have them.

If this changes

Should Brainy ever add cloud backup of a child's progress, that would be opt-in, would require a parent's explicit consent, and this notice would be updated before it launched. It would not be switched on quietly for existing users. A licence is not that: it holds a grown-up's email address and nothing about a child, and it never uploads a child's work.

Your rights

Almost nothing personal reaches us, so for the most part there is nothing for us to disclose or correct on your behalf — you hold all of it, on your device, and can export or destroy it at any time.

Your email address, if you gave us one, is the exception: that is personal data, it is yours, and you have the usual rights over it. Write to the address below and we will tell you exactly what we hold, correct it, or delete it. Deleting it ends the licence attached to it, so we will confirm that with you first rather than quietly closing subjects your child is using.

Erasure is the exception, and it is handled in the app rather than by writing to us: turning off Share anonymous usage data, or using Delete everything, deletes the usage records held under your code. We would rather you did not have to ask us, because asking us would mean telling us who you are. If something has gone wrong, or you have a question, contact us at the address below.

Your rights in full

Under the NDPA — and the UK GDPR where it applies to you — you have the right to be told what we hold about you, to get a copy of it, to have it corrected, to have it erased, to object to what we are doing with it, to restrict it while a dispute is resolved, and to take it elsewhere in a portable form. You also have the right not to be subject to a decision made purely by a machine that significantly affects you; we make no such decisions.

Exercising any of them costs nothing and takes one email. We will not ask you to prove who you are beyond replying from the address we already hold, because demanding a copy of somebody's ID in order to honour a privacy request collects more than the request itself.
