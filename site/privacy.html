Privacy notice

Last updated 4 August 2026

By default Brainy collects nothing. Your child's answers, progress and report stay in the browser on your device and are never uploaded. During setup we ask whether we may collect anonymous usage data; the box starts unticked, and if you leave it that way nothing is ever sent. Nothing about your child ever leaves the tablet, on any plan.

What we collect

Nothing at all unless you opt in, and never anything about your child. There is no account for a child and no login anywhere in the app.

There is one exception, and it belongs to the grown-up rather than the child: if you claim a free place, redeem a code or buy a licence, we store your email address, and your name and phone number if you gave them. That is what lets your access be restored on a new tablet, and it is the only reason it exists. Maths is free for everybody with no sign-up at all, so you can use Brainy indefinitely without ever telling us anything.

What is stored on your device

So that the app can remember where your child got to, the following is saved in your browser's local storage — on your device only:

  • The first name and age you entered, and the class and curriculum you chose
  • Which questions have been answered, and how well, per skill
  • Coins, level, streak, badges, and the characters and pets collected
  • A short history of recent sessions, including questions answered incorrectly
  • Your settings, including the four-digit grown-up code

You can see all of it, export it as a file, remove one child, or delete everything — including anything shared with us — from the grown-up area. Clearing your browser data also removes it.

What leaves your device

Nothing, unless you choose one of these three things:

  • You export a backup file and move it somewhere yourself. The file holds your children's progress and your PIN, but never the sharing id — consent stays with the device it was given on, so a restored tablet asks you again.
  • You send the anonymous summary described below, either once or weekly.
  • You write to us using the feedback form.
  • You claim a free place, enter a code, or buy a licence — which sends your email address and the code, and nothing else. See Sign-ups, licences and payments below.

With all of these switched off and untouched — which is the default — Brainy makes no network requests at all after the page has loaded. That is also why it works offline, and it stays true of the child-facing part of the app on every plan.

Anonymous usage data, if you opt in

If you tick the box during setup — or switch it on later in the grown-up area — Brainy sends us a small amount of information so we can tell whether it is working:

  • That Brainy was set up, and which curriculum and class you chose
  • That it was opened on a given day, and how many children use this device
  • For each quest: the subject, how many questions, how many were right, and how long it took
  • A weekly summary of which topics are scoring worst
  • A random code, generated at the moment you opt in, so one tablet is not counted twice

It never includes your child's name, their age, or anything they typed.

About that random code

We want to be straight about this rather than call it "anonymous" and leave it there. The code does not identify your child, is not linked to a name or an email, and cannot be resolved to a person. But because it stays the same between visits, it lets us see that the same device came back — and under Nigerian and European data protection law that makes it pseudonymous data, not fully anonymous data. That is precisely why we ask for your consent first rather than assuming it.

The code is created only when you opt in, and deleted the moment you opt out. Before you agree, there is no identifier in Brainy at all.

Changing your mind

Grown-up area → Settings → Share anonymous usage data. Switching it off stops all sending immediately, asks us to delete everything already received under your code, and then deletes the code from your device. Deletion happens first and on purpose: the code is the only handle on those records, so once it is gone nothing could find them again.

If your tablet is offline at that moment the sending still stops, but the earlier records will remain — they carry no name, no contact details and no date beyond the day, and nothing will ever be added to them. The app tells you which of the two happened rather than assuming. Delete everything in the same screen does the same erasure and additionally wipes the whole app back to a fresh install.

How we learn whether the app works

Because we collect nothing by default, the only way we find out that a question is badly worded or a topic is too hard is if you tell us. In the grown-up area you can read the exact summary, then copy it, send it once, or switch on Send it automatically each week. That switch is off until you turn it on, and you can turn it off again at any time.

That summary deliberately contains:

  • The curriculum and class, and rough bucketed counts (for example "100–249 questions")
  • Overall accuracy, and which of our topics had the lowest scores

It deliberately does not contain:

  • Any name, device identifier or account of any kind
  • Any dates or timestamps
  • Any question text, or anything your child typed

We are being careful about the difference between de-identified and anonymous. A persistent identifier plus a detailed history would still be personal data even with the name removed, because it describes one individual. The summary avoids that by carrying no identifier and no timeline at all. Nothing is sent automatically, and nothing is sent unless you choose to send it.

The feedback form

The grown-up area has a feedback form. Whatever you type in it is sent to us, along with the category you picked and — only if you tick the box — the anonymous summary. An email address is optional and used solely to reply to you. Please do not include your child's name or other personal details; we do not need them and ask you not to send them.

Sign-ups, licences and payments

Mathematics — your child's class and every earlier class as revision — is free permanently, for everybody, with no sign-up and no card. The other subjects are covered by a licence, and the first twenty families keep everything free for ever.

We email you three things and nothing else: your access code, a confirmation if you paid, and a warning about a week before a licence runs out. No newsletter, no offers, no reminders to play.

When you claim a place, enter a code, or buy a licence, we store:

  • Your email address, and your name and phone number if you chose to give them
  • Which plan you are on, when it started, and when it runs out
  • Your family access code, and how many tablets have used it
  • For a purchase: the amount, the currency, a payment reference and whether it succeeded

We do not store card details — we never see them. Nothing about your child is attached to any of it: no name, no age, no answers, no progress. The link between a licence and a child is the code on your tablet, and it stops there.

Your access code is checked with us about once a week when the tablet is online, which also records that the tablet used it — that is how we notice a code being passed around a hundred families. If we cannot be reached, nothing changes and your child keeps playing.

You can remove the licence from a device at any time in the grown-up area, under Access. That closes the paid subjects on that tablet and cancels nothing — entering the code again reopens them.

Third parties

Brainy contains no analytics service, no advertising, no tracking pixels, no third-party fonts and no embedded content. Usage data, summaries and feedback go to our own server and are not shared with anyone else.

There are two, and only if you have given us an email address:

  • Resend deliver our email — your access code, a payment confirmation, and a warning before a licence runs out. They process your address in order to deliver those messages and for nothing else. We do not run a mailing list, and there is nothing here to unsubscribe from: every message is about your own access.
  • Paystack take the payment, and only if you buy a licence. You go to their page to enter your card details, and they hold those details and the receipt — we receive only your email address, an amount and whether it worked.

Both have their own privacy notices covering what they do with what they are given. Neither receives anything about your child, because nothing about your child exists on our side to send them.

Advertising

There is no advertising in Brainy, and there will not be any in future versions.

Children's safety

Brainy has no chat, no messaging, no user-generated content and no way for a child to communicate with anyone. There are no external links anywhere in the child-facing part of the app. The only place that can navigate away is the grown-up area, which is behind a code.

Hosting and where data is kept

The site is served as static files by Vercel, our hosting provider, who may keep standard server logs including IP addresses for security and reliability. We do not read those logs to build any profile, and they are not joined to anything above.

Anything you do choose to send — usage data, summaries, feedback — is stored in our own database and is not shared with, or sold to, anyone. We do not use a third-party analytics service.

How long we keep it

Usage data is kept for as long as it is useful for improving Brainy and then deleted, or until you delete it yourself from the grown-up area, whichever comes first. Because it carries no name or contact details, it cannot be traced back to a family in any case. Feedback that includes an email address is kept only until the conversation is finished.

Sign-up and licence records are kept while the licence is live, because they are what proves it exists — and for a free-forever family that is indefinitely, which is the point of the promise. Payment records are kept for as long as tax and accounting rules require. Ask us and we will delete a licence record; it ends the access it was holding, so we will say so before we do it.

If this changes

Should Brainy ever add cloud backup of a child's progress, that would be opt-in, would require a parent's explicit consent, and this notice would be updated before it launched. It would not be switched on quietly for existing users. A licence is not that: it holds a grown-up's email address and nothing about a child, and it never uploads a child's work.

Your rights

Almost nothing personal reaches us, so for the most part there is nothing for us to disclose or correct on your behalf — you hold all of it, on your device, and can export or destroy it at any time.

Your email address, if you gave us one, is the exception: that is personal data, it is yours, and you have the usual rights over it. Write to the address below and we will tell you exactly what we hold, correct it, or delete it. Deleting it ends the licence attached to it, so we will confirm that with you first rather than quietly closing subjects your child is using.

Erasure is the exception, and it is handled in the app rather than by writing to us: turning off Share anonymous usage data, or using Delete everything, deletes the usage records held under your code. We would rather you did not have to ask us, because asking us would mean telling us who you are. If something has gone wrong, or you have a question, contact us at the address below.
